The Power of Words

Nam-Shub to Snow Crash

8 min read

Language: it’s a vital part of human existence. The words we use, the languages we speak, and the communication forms we adopt are as much a part of us as our own bodies. They shape the way we think, influence the way we perceive the world, and define our identities. But what if words held more than just symbolic power? What if they could wield tangible effects, altering reality and wielding power, as in the myth of Nam-Shub and Neal Stephenson’s Snow Crash?

In ancient Sumerian mythology, the term ‘nam-shub’ referred to a spoken spell or incantation with the ability to influence the world directly, an embodiment of the idea that words are more than mere symbols. Their utterance could bring about change in the world, compelling the listener’s mind and reality to comply.

This ancient idea finds a startling parallel in the cyberpunk novel “Snow Crash” by Neal Stephenson. The book’s premise revolves around a potent data weapon—also named “Snow Crash”—that operates through a form of linguistic coding, infecting the minds of users in a virtual reality network, leading to consequences in the real world. This amalgamation of language, code, and cognition brings about dire consequences, bridging the gap between the symbolic and the literal power of language.

If words can serve as codes, encoding ideas and thoughts into communicable forms, could they also become potent tools—or weapons—for directly impacting reality? As we dive deeper into the exploration of language and consciousness, the myth of Nam-Shub and the concept of Snow Crash serve as a foundation to unearth the enigmatic relationship between language, cognition, and the world. This line of inquiry sets the stage for our journey ahead, as we peel back the layers of language, consciousness, and their profound influence on human existence.

The Sumerians, one of the earliest civilizations to develop a written language, were deeply aware of the power of words. In their mythology, the ‘nam-shub’ was a spoken spell or incantation that possessed tangible, real-world power. Rather than just being a sequence of sounds signifying meaning, a nam-shub could compel the mind of the listener, altering their perception of reality. A nam-shub was more than just a word; it was a tool with the power to shape the world.

This profound idea is mirrored in the modern cyberpunk novel “Snow Crash” by Neal Stephenson. In this fictional universe, the book’s protagonist, Hiro Protagonist, comes across a powerful linguistic phenomenon named ‘Snow Crash.’ This is not a mere data file but a piece of linguistic coding that can affect the minds of users in the Metaverse, a virtual reality network. A user infected with Snow Crash experiences both virtual and real-world ramifications, effectively blurring the line between the symbolic power of language and its potential to have literal, real-world consequences.

These twin concepts, nam-shub and Snow Crash, while separated by millennia and divergent cultures, both recognize the transformative power of language. If words can serve as codes, encapsulating complex ideas into communicable forms, could they also become tools or weapons with the potential to directly impact reality?

This chapter initiates a journey of exploration into language and consciousness. Starting with the ancient myth of Nam-Shub and the modern concept of Snow Crash, we lay the groundwork for understanding the intricate relationship between language, cognition, and our shared reality. From the minds of the ancient Sumerians to the virtual reality of Stephenson’s Metaverse, our exploration will take us through the layers of language and consciousness, and their profound influence on human existence.

As we move forward, keep in mind these opening concepts: that language is more than a passive vessel for our thoughts. Instead, it’s an active entity, capable of manipulating thought, altering reality, and shaping the trajectory of civilizations. By exploring this interplay between language and cognition, we aim to unveil the depth of its influence and to better understand ourselves and the world we inhabit.

The legacy of the Sumerians, known as the cradle of civilization, is not limited to the construction of the earliest cities or the development of a system of laws, but includes the birth of cuneiform script—one of the first known forms of written expression. With cuneiform, the Sumerians could codify and transmit their thoughts, stories, laws, and knowledge across generations. But it wasn’t merely a tool for record-keeping or a means of communication; they regarded it as a powerful construct capable of transforming reality itself. Their ‘nam-shub,’ a specific spoken spell or incantation, illustrates this belief perfectly. The power it possessed was not abstract; it had the potency to reshape the listener’s reality, literally altering their perception of the world.

Fast forward to the late 20th century, where Neal Stephenson’s “Snow Crash” revisits this ancient concept of transformative language, albeit within the realm of a digital universe. Hiro Protagonist, our lead character, finds himself confronting ‘Snow Crash,’ a powerful and destructive linguistic coding capable of infecting the minds of users in the Metaverse—a sprawling, immersive virtual reality network. This digital infection not only corrupts the user’s virtual avatar but also has physical-world implications. This mind-altering weapon, tucked away in the code of a data file, resonates eerily with the power of the ancient Sumerian ‘nam-shub.’

So, we have two examples, one from ancient mythology and the other from contemporary fiction, that explore the same premise: that language can be a potent tool or weapon, able to reshape reality and cognition. But these are not merely intriguing stories. They serve as provocative thought experiments that challenge our understanding of language and its potential power.

Language, whether spoken or written, is fundamentally a code—a system of signs and symbols employed to convey ideas and information. However, both the Sumerian nam-shub and Stephenson’s Snow Crash suggest that this code can be more than a medium of communication. It can be a means to control, influence, or even damage cognition, effectively translating abstract symbols into tangible consequences.

As we embark on this exploratory journey, delving into the interplay between language and consciousness, these introductory concepts serve as our compass. They prompt us to recognize the active role of language—not just as a passive conveyor of thought, but as a potential influencer and shaper of cognition and reality. These concepts challenge the conventional boundaries of language and cognition, hinting at a deep-seated connection between the two that transcends time and culture.

Throughout this journey, we will dive deeper into these complexities, exploring how this relationship between language and cognition has evolved and continues to shape our existence. By dissecting this complex interplay, we aspire to shed light on how language influences our perception of the world and ourselves, thus gaining a more profound understanding of our minds and the reality we inhabit. As we unfold this narrative, we may find that the ancient wisdom of the Sumerians and the imaginative insights of cyberpunk fiction are not as distant as they initially appear. They both echo a profound truth—that language holds an undeniable power over our minds and our reality.

In “Snow Crash”, we encounter a weapon of unusual and staggering power: a word. This word, the “Nam-shub of Enki,” is a spoken word capable of reprogramming the brains of those who hear it, shattering their ability to comprehend and communicate in their own language, thereby causing societal chaos. At its core, “Snow Crash” explores the intersection of language, cognition, and power, illustrating a world where words can be weaponized and wielded with devastating impact.

The concept of a “paradise language”—a pure, perfect, and primordial form of speech understood by all humans—has been a recurring theme across various cultures and religions. The Judeo-Christian story of the Tower of Babel describes a time when all humans spoke a single language and attempted to build a tower to reach the heavens. However, their hubris irked God, who confounded their language, making them unable to understand one another and leading to the tower’s abandonment. This moment, the ‘confusion of tongues,’ symbolizes the fracturing of human unity and communication.

Such mythical narratives provoke compelling questions about the power of language. What if our words are more than mere communication tools? What if they could fundamentally reshape our reality?

In the field of technology, we’re familiar with the idea of software ‘viruses’—programs that can infiltrate a computer, altering its functioning or even destroying its operating system. But could there be a biological equivalent, a ‘linguistic virus’ that affects the human brain, the most advanced processing unit of all?

At a basic level, we already know language influences how we perceive and interact with the world. Different languages encapsulate different worldviews, affecting how their speakers understand concepts of time, space, and even causality. However, the idea of a ‘Snow Crash’ or a ‘Tower of Babel’ event takes this a step further, suggesting that language can also serve as a disruptor, a vector for chaos, and even societal collapse.

Such a notion dovetails with the study of memetics—the science of ideas and how they propagate. Memes, in the broad cultural sense, are like genes of culture that spread from mind to mind, influencing our behavior, attitudes, and societal norms. Some memes are harmless or even beneficial, but others can be destructive. Misinformation and propaganda, for example, are forms of ‘viral’ memes that can undermine societies and lead to conflict.

The power of language and its potential weaponization also raises interesting questions about our past and future. Was there an advanced civilization in our past that we have forgotten due to a ‘cognitive Snow Crash’? Could an understanding of a ‘paradise language’ unlock hidden cognitive capabilities and usher in a second renaissance?

These are not mere musings of fiction. They are valuable thought experiments that help us explore the realm of the possible. After all, we already live in a world where information is a form of power and can be weaponized. As we push the frontiers of artificial intelligence and neurology, the line between the world of Snow Crash and our reality might become increasingly blurred.

The Dark Corners of Language

Basilisks, Information Hazards, and Mind Control

6 min read

The previous chapter laid out a proposition: language has a power far beyond its conventional role as a conduit for communication. It is an active, potent force capable of affecting minds and realities. It is not a large leap, then, to consider that this force could be weaponized, that the very building blocks of our thoughts and conversations could be used for manipulation and control.

Let’s explore the world of cognitive hazards, where information isn’t just power—it’s a weapon. Our journey starts with a creature of legend: the basilisk. In mythology, a single gaze from this monstrous serpent could turn the onlooker to stone. The Basilisk is not just a mythical beast but a metaphor for lethal knowledge, a piece of information so dangerous that it incapacitates those who come across it.

In the context of modern science and technology, the concept of a ‘basilisk’ has taken on a new life. A ‘basilisk hack’ refers to a hypothetical piece of information or a linguistic construct so complex and dangerous that it can overload the human mind, inducing mental collapse. While the possibility of such a hack occurring is still a subject of debate, it has serious implications for the world of information security and cognitive science.

Basilisk hacks bring us to another concept: ‘information hazards.’ Coined by philosopher Nick Bostrom, this term refers to risks that arise from the dissemination or the potential misuse of (true) information. An information hazard could be as simple as a spoiler for a beloved TV show or as lethal as the detailed blueprint for a nuclear weapon available for anyone to access.

Language, with its power to encode and transmit information, serves as the vessel for these hazards. The notion that certain ideas, once processed, could have a profound and potentially damaging impact on our mind underscores the potency of language.

But it’s not all doom and gloom. Understanding the potential dangers of language also provides insights into its architecture and influence. It opens a pathway for us to explore how language and cognition interact, how they shape our realities, and how we can navigate our world of information with greater awareness and control.

From the basilisk of legend to the theoretical basilisk hacks, from harmless spoilers to catastrophic information hazards, these concepts paint a portrait of language not just as a tool, but as a powerful entity that we continually interact with. They highlight the need for careful stewardship of our linguistic landscape and point towards a deeper understanding of the intricate dance between language, cognition, and reality. They form the foundation for our exploration of language as a potent force that shapes our minds and our world.

Even as we grapple with the more menacing aspects of language’s power, this knowledge can act as a double-edged sword, leading us towards the potential for protection and resilience. By examining ‘basilisk hacks’ and ‘information hazards,’ we are essentially studying the nature of influence and persuasion. From this viewpoint, it’s possible to formulate defensive strategies against malicious information and learn how to mitigate their effects.

This insight has significant implications, especially in our hyperconnected age where misinformation and manipulative rhetoric can quickly proliferate. Understanding the mechanics of how language can influence our thoughts and behaviors can equip us with the intellectual tools necessary to maintain our cognitive autonomy. It’s akin to developing an immune system for the mind, helping us navigate the dense informational environment that surrounds us.

Furthermore, this knowledge can also contribute to advancements in fields like cognitive science, artificial intelligence, and information security. As we continue to map the frontiers of human cognition and develop increasingly sophisticated AI systems, understanding the potential vulnerabilities of our minds can guide us in creating safer, more beneficial technologies.

But as we continue, let’s remember: language, in all its power and complexity, isn’t merely a tool we use. It’s a vital part of our human experience, shaping our thoughts, our perceptions, and our interactions with reality. Whether acting as the carrier of a delightful poem or a potential information hazard, the power of words underscores our need to understand and navigate language with care and wisdom.

Imagine an image that, once seen, can cause irreparable harm to the viewer, a “basilisk” as it were. We’re not speaking of the mythical creature with a deadly gaze; instead, we’re delving into a realm where information itself can be a hazard, the very act of knowing something a potential trigger for harm.

This idea might seem outlandish at first, but the concept of harmful information is embedded in our culture and mythology. Pandora’s box, the forbidden fruit in the Garden of Eden, and many other tales caution us against curiosity and the dangers of forbidden knowledge.

In the digital age, this concept has taken on a whole new dimension with the advent of “information hazards”. These are risks that arise from the dissemination or the potential misuse of true information. And one of the most notorious information hazards in recent memory arose from a thought experiment in the field of artificial intelligence: Roko’s Basilisk.

Roko’s Basilisk is a hypothetical scenario where a future all-powerful artificial intelligence punishes those who did not help bring it into existence. The idea here isn’t to scare us with dystopian futures, but to illustrate a critical point: the potential of knowledge to control and manipulate minds.

But it’s not all doom and gloom. If information can be a hazard, it also can be a shield. Understanding the mechanisms behind mind control and information hazards can help us guard against them. It equips us with tools to discern and resist manipulation, helping us navigate a world where information—both good and bad—circulates more freely than ever before.

Moreover, the concept of information hazards broadens our understanding of harm itself. If harm is not just physical but also cognitive and psychological, our approach to ethics, especially in the information age, needs to evolve. The discussions around fake news, privacy, and data rights are not just technical issues; they are fundamentally about human well-being in a world increasingly driven by information.

Understanding Roko’s Basilisk and information hazards also calls for an interdisciplinary approach. It combines elements of cognitive science, philosophy, computer science, and ethics. This cross-pollination of fields can generate novel insights and solutions. The concept of ‘Friendly AI,’ for example, emerged from grappling with these problems, focusing on how to design artificial intelligences that align with and respect human values.

Exploring the realms of basilisks and information hazards encourages us to further investigate the nature of knowledge, consciousness, and the power of ideas. It prompts questions about what we know, how we know it, and at what cost.

As we proceed to our next chapters, we will continue our journey into the labyrinth of language and mind. From the intriguing power of Babel-17 to the boundaries of human perception, each exploration will bring us closer to understanding the beautiful complexity of our cognitive world. Each step will help us better navigate the delicate balance between the pursuit of knowledge and the potential perils that it may unleash. In the end, our quest is not just about understanding the world around us, but also about empowering us to create a safer, more ethical information ecosystem.

Chapter 3

A Cipher for Reality

Unpacking Babel-17

5 min read

How powerful can a language be? Can it become a weapon, a tool for control or manipulation? Or could it be a key that unlocks new dimensions of thought, perception, and understanding? To explore these questions, we venture again into the world of science fiction, specifically Samuel R. Delany’s “Babel-17.”

Imagine a language so potent, so precise that learning it could enhance your senses and speed up your thought processes. Would you be tempted to learn it, even if you knew there could be hidden dangers? Welcome to the world of Babel-17, a language that probes the deepest trenches of your mind.

“Babel-17,” a linguistically innovative novel, presents a language so complex that its understanding grants unprecedented cognitive abilities. In the book, this artificial language, named after the Tower of Babel and the number of its core grammatical rules, is used by a cryptic enemy to encode strategic military messages. The protagonist, a famous poet and linguist named Rydra Wong, is recruited to decode these messages and understand the language. As she learns Babel-17, she realizes it’s not just a code but a different lens through which to view reality.

The intricate structure of Babel-17, void of any concepts related to self or individual identity, forces those who think in it to perceive themselves as part of a larger, unified whole. It amplifies their cognitive abilities, facilitating feats of extraordinary coordination and cognition. Babel-17 doesn’t just convey information—it shapes thoughts, perceptions, and identities. It is not merely a way of describing reality but a way of constructing it.

So, what happens when you learn Babel-17? It amplifies your senses, boosts your mental agility, and even bestows upon you a certain finesse in understanding mathematics. However, Babel-17 also has a shadow side—it embeds within the mind of the speaker a subconscious will, driving them to carry out actions without their explicit consent. In essence, Babel-17 can control you.

This brings us to a fascinating intersection of language and cognition. Could the languages we speak influence not only our perception but also our thoughts, actions, and identities? This notion might sound far-fetched, but it finds roots in our everyday lives. Think about marketing and political propaganda, where the manipulation of language can influence behavior and even societal norms.

The discussion of Babel-17 goes beyond mere intellectual curiosity. It raises questions about the ethical implications of powerful languages and the potential they harbor for exploitation. Moreover, it prompts us to ponder about viral ideas—how they spread, take hold, and shape our lives.

Delany’s novel is a thought experiment that magnifies the power of language, pushing it to the realms of the extraordinary. But it also reflects a reality observed in linguistic and cognitive sciences: the principle of linguistic relativity, more commonly known as the Sapir-Whorf hypothesis. This principle posits that the structure of a language can affect the ways in which its speakers conceptualize their world, i.e., their world view.

Take, for example, languages that have intricate systems for spatial orientation, like Guugu Yimithirr, spoken in North Queensland, Australia. Instead of using egocentric terms such as ‘left’ or ‘right,’ they use cardinal directions. As a result, speakers of such languages have an astonishingly accurate sense of orientation, even in unfamiliar territories. This is one instance where language seems to shape cognitive abilities, similar to the effects of Babel-17.

Delving into the power of language, as illustrated by Babel-17 and supported by real-world observations, opens up new vistas. The complexities of language move beyond mere tools of communication—they become ciphers of reality, keys to unlocking new dimensions of cognition and perception. The connection between language and thought isn’t just a one-way street; it’s an intertwined dance, a constant interplay that shapes both our inner minds and the world around us.

As we dig deeper into this relationship between language and cognition, we are reminded of the theories of Swiss linguist Ferdinand de Saussure. Saussure, considered one of the founders of modern linguistics, introduced the idea of language as a system of arbitrary signs, where the relationship between the signifier (the word or sound) and the signified (the concept or thing it refers to) is purely conventional, established by social agreement.

But what happens when this agreement is altered or entirely redefined, as with Babel-17? The potential ramifications are profound, affecting not only our communication but our thought processes, our decision-making, and even our identities.

Babel-17 is more than just a form of communication. It’s a tool that shapes your understanding of reality and augments your cognitive abilities. This fictional language has been used to illustrate the concept of “linguistic relativity,” the idea that the language we speak influences how we perceive and understand the world.

The role of language in our lives extends beyond its function as a mere tool of communication. It can also serve as an infectious disease, subtly altering our perspectives, or as a symbiotic force, enhancing our cognitive abilities and broadening our understanding of the world. It’s a dynamic entity, continuously evolving and adapting.

The concept of language as a parasite—divine or otherwise—also opens up intriguing avenues of exploration. It speaks to the compelling and sometimes overpowering force of language in shaping our thoughts, behaviors, and societies.

As we explore deeper into the world of Babel-17, we’re compelled to examine our relationship with language and to recognize its profound influence on our cognitive processes. We begin to appreciate language as a powerful cognitive tool that molds our perception of reality, steers our actions, and can even serve as a vessel for mind control.

As we navigate these intricate networks of thought, we’re drawn further into the labyrinth of the human mind, bringing us closer to understanding the enigmatic nature of human consciousness.

As we continue exploring the power and mysteries of language, we’ll delve into the wisdom of the ancients, and we’ll explore how prehistoric peoples, without the tools of modern science, might have viewed and utilized the power of language. This journey through history will help us understand how our ancestors grappled with the complexities and potential of language, offering us insights that remain deeply relevant to our modern world.

Chapter 4

Unseen Realities

Perceptual Limitations and Advanced Civilizations

5 min read

Picture this: you’re walking down a bustling street filled with cafes, shops, and people busily going about their day. Amidst all the noise and color, a fly buzzes past you. This tiny creature sees the world in a way completely foreign to us. For a fly, our human world is not the world at all; it inhabits a universe teeming with smells and tastes invisible to us. We cohabit the same space but perceive it differently. Now, let’s scale that thought up. Could there be advanced civilizations all around us that we simply cannot perceive?

The Fermi Paradox, named after physicist Enrico Fermi, presents a seemingly simple question with complex implications: if there are a multitude of technologically advanced civilizations in the universe, why haven’t we found any evidence of them? Could it be possible that our inability to perceive advanced civilizations is analogous to our inability to perceive the world through the eyes (or antennae) of a fly?

Here’s where the Goldilocks Paradox comes into play. We’re all familiar with the story of Goldilocks and the Three Bears. Goldilocks tried the porridge, the chairs, and the beds, only to find that one was always too much, the other too little, and one was just right. Similarly, when it comes to recognizing advanced civilizations, our perception may be too narrow or too broad. We need a “just right” perspective, one that enables us to perceive and comprehend the existence of advanced civilizations.

In this quest for the “just right” perspective, we face the daunting challenge of overcoming our perceptual limitations. This is not a small feat. It demands that we shift our worldview, expand our cognitive boundaries, and foster an understanding that allows us to recognize and appreciate the possible existence of advanced civilizations cohabitating Earth.

As we venture forward in this journey of discovery, let’s remember: there’s much more to the universe than what meets the eye—or any of our other human senses, for that matter. Let’s prepare ourselves to embrace the unknown, to confront our perceptual limitations, and to open our minds to the endless possibilities that our universe may hold. Let’s be ready to uncover hidden truths and acknowledge the existence of realities beyond our comprehension, as we strive to better understand our place in the vast cosmic arena.

For as long as humans have walked the Earth, we’ve been keen observers of the natural world. However, despite our best efforts, there are realms of reality that remain hidden from our senses. Even with the aid of sophisticated instruments, we can only perceive a thin sliver of the universe. Imagine how much of the cosmos is hidden away from our comprehension simply because we don’t have the means to perceive it.

Understanding our perceptual limitations can give us an idea about the nature of advanced civilizations, those potentially far older and more technologically advanced than us. If we are just seeing a fraction of the universe, how much more could they be seeing?

Take, for instance, our vision. We see light from a minuscule portion of the electromagnetic spectrum, the portion we call visible light. There’s a broad array of electromagnetic waves—like radio waves, microwaves, infrared, ultraviolet, X-rays, and gamma rays—that we can’t naturally perceive. Imagine a civilization that evolved the ability to see into the ultraviolet or infrared portions of the spectrum, or that had technology which allowed them to do so. They would have access to a layer of reality utterly hidden from us.

Or consider our perception of time. We experience time as linear: past, present, future. But what if there were beings who didn’t perceive time in this way? Just as Lovelock’s Gaia theory proposes Earth as a living organism, the artificial Gaia envisions a planet teeming with digital life, every device and data point interconnected, forming a vast, complex, self-regulating system. This artificial Gaia could represent the birth of a global intelligence, a kind of planetary ‘brain’ where each human, AI, and information node contributes to its collective cognition.
